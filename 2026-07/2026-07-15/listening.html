Script

Every morning, I get to work by driving over a long ridge that looks down onto the valley where the old oil refinery sits. From up there, the towers and pipes look almost peaceful, glowing faintly in the early light.

My uncle worked at that refinery for thirty years, and he always said the real danger was invisible. You could not see the gases that sometimes leaked from a tired old pipe, and that, he told me, was exactly why the workers had to keep the pressure down at all times and check every reading twice.

He never seemed frightened by the job, though. He used to say that people who worked with something invisible and dangerous every day learned to respect it quietly, without fear taking over their lives. Loud panic, he said, helped nobody; a calm, careful habit did.

Now, whenever I drive over that ridge on my way to the office, I think of him checking his instruments one more time before going home. It is a strange kind of comfort, knowing that somewhere behind that quiet, glowing view, someone is still keeping something invisible safely under control.

私のおじは、その精製所で30年働いていました。彼はいつも、本当の危険は目に見えないものだと言っていました。時々古びたパイプから漏れるガスは見えません。だからこそ、作業員は常に圧力を低く抑え、あらゆる数値を二度確認しなければならなかったのだと、彼は私に教えてくれました。

それでも彼は、その仕事を怖がっているようには見えませんでした。彼はよく、目に見えない危険なものと毎日向き合う人は、恐怖に人生を乗っ取らせることなく、それを静かに敬うようになるのだと言っていました。大きな声のパニックは誰の役にも立たず、落ち着いた注意深い習慣こそが役立つ、と彼は言いました。

今、通勤の途中であの尾根を車で越えるたびに、私は彼が家に帰る前にもう一度計器を確認している姿を思い出します。あの静かに輝く景色の裏側で、誰かが今も目に見えない何かを安全に制御し続けている——それは不思議な種類の安心感です。
