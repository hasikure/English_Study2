My first night shift at the hospital was one I will never forget. I had just started as a volunteer, and my job was simple: push a trolley of meals and clean sheets from ward to ward, quietly, without disturbing the sleeping patients.

For the first hour, everything went smoothly. Then, just after midnight, a heavy storm hit. The lights flickered, and for about thirty seconds the power was completely cut off. In the sudden darkness, with my trolley stuck between two doors, I honestly did not know what to do.

The nurses, though, were astonishingly calm. They had trained for every conceivable emergency, and within moments the backup power came on and everyone carried on as if nothing had happened. One of them told me gently to keep worried visitors at a distance from the recovery room, so the patients could rest.

By the end of the shift, at six in the morning, I was exhausted but strangely happy. I had done nothing heroic — just pushed a trolley and stayed out of the way. Yet watching those nurses work through the storm taught me more about quiet courage than any book ever had. I signed up for another shift the very next week.
