Script

For most of my life, I have eaten far too quickly. As a child I was always the first to finish, and I was quietly proud of it. It was only last month that I started to wonder whether this old habit was doing me any good.

A friend of mine, who works as a nurse, explained the problem simply. The stomach needs time to digest a meal, she said, and eating fast gives it no chance to catch up. She suggested a small experiment: chew each mouthful about double the number of times I normally would, and put my fork down between bites.

From now on, I decided, I would at least try. The result honestly astonished me. Slowing down did not just help my stomach; it changed the whole meal. I noticed flavours I had been swallowing without tasting for years. I also felt full much sooner, on far less food than usual.

It sounds almost too simple to matter. But that tiny change — chewing slowly, pausing, paying attention — has quietly improved my evenings more than any expensive diet ever did. Sometimes the most useful advice is the kind your grandmother could have given you for free.
