At the outset of our summer holiday, things did not go smoothly. We arrived at the ferry terminal two hours early, only to find an enormous queue already stretching out of the door. Worse, we were short of cash, and the machine that sold tickets accepted only coins.

I left my sister with the bags and joined the slow line, expecting a miserable morning. My little niece, who is only three, sat on my sister's lap and quickly fell asleep, completely unbothered by the noise and the crowd.

Then something unexpected happened. The woman standing behind me in the queue noticed our problem with the coins and, to my surprise, simply handed me the change I needed. She refused to take my paper money in return. "Someone did the same for me once," she said, and shrugged.

The queue, which had looked hopeless, moved faster than we feared, and we caught our ferry with minutes to spare. Standing on the deck later, watching the harbour shrink behind us, I kept thinking about that stranger. She will never know how much her small kindness meant. Sometimes a holiday is saved not by planning, but by the quiet generosity of people you never see again.

私は姉に荷物を預けて、そののろい列に加わり、みじめな午前になるだろうと思っていました。まだ3歳の幼い姪は、姉のひざの上に座って、騒音や人ごみにまったく動じることなく、すぐに眠ってしまいました。

そのとき、思いがけないことが起こりました。行列で私の後ろに立っていた女性が、私たちの硬貨の問題に気づき、驚いたことに、私が必要としていた小銭をただ手渡してくれたのです。彼女はお返しの紙幣を受け取ろうとしませんでした。「昔、誰かが私に同じことをしてくれたの」と言って、肩をすくめました。
