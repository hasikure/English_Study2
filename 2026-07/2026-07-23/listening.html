Last summer I worked as a volunteer lifeguard at our local swimming pool, and the whole job turned out to be far more straightforward than I expected. By regulation, we had to check the water depth markers every morning and count the swimmers every fifteen minutes, but beyond that, most of the job was simply watching quietly.

The busiest hour was always right after school, when a group of children would run in shouting and immediately start a loud game of tag around the shallow end. For the first few weeks, the excitement of a new summer job kept me completely alert to every splash and shout. Then, as August arrived, I noticed that feeling slowly begin to wear off. The days blurred together, and I had to remind myself that being calm did not mean being careless.

One quiet Tuesday, a small boy nearly slipped near the deep end while chasing a friend. I reached him in seconds, and afterwards I felt oddly grateful for all those boring, repetitive checks. They were, I realised, exactly what entitled me to trust my own reactions in that single important moment. Routine is rarely exciting, but on that Tuesday, it was the only thing that mattered.
