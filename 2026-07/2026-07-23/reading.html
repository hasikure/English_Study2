English Text

The first time I watched an albatross climb to a great altitude above the open ocean, gliding for hours without a single wingbeat, I felt a genuine thrill that had nothing to do with excitement in the usual sense. It felt closer to awe. What struck me even more, once I learned about the bird's life, was not the flight itself but the quiet, decades-long loyalty behind it.

Albatrosses, like a small number of other seabirds, typically choose one partner and stay with that partner, in effect, for life. In the plainest sense, they are married to each other long before humans invented the word for it. Every year, the same two birds return to the same narrow cliff, greet each other with a strange dance, and gently touch beak to beak, a small ritual that researchers believe helps strengthen the bond before the difficult work of raising a chick begins.

Sailors who overheard these courtship calls centuries ago carried the stories home, and some historians believe such tales quietly shaped our own romantic imagination, long before anyone thought to send a paper valentine to a sweetheart on a particular February afternoon. The image of two creatures choosing each other, once, for good, proved far more moving than any single afternoon of courtship.

We are mortal in a way an albatross is not spared either, and yet we often manage our human loyalties far worse than these birds manage theirs. We drift, we compare, we wonder whether some other partner might have been more exciting. The albatross, as far as anyone can tell, does none of this. It simply returns, year after year, to the same narrow cliff and the same familiar beak.

None of this means devotion should be blind or unquestioning. But there is something worth learning from a creature that treats commitment not as a single dramatic promise but as a habit, repeated quietly, season after season, at real cost, until death. We call ourselves the more advanced species. On this particular point, the evidence is not entirely on our side.

アホウドリは、ごく少数の他の海鳥と同じく、たいてい一羽の相手を選び、事実上、生涯その相手と添い遂げる。最も率直な意味で言えば、人間がその言葉を発明するずっと前から、彼らは互いに「結婚している」のだ。毎年、同じ2羽が同じ狭い崖に戻ってきて、奇妙な踊りで挨拶を交わし、そっとくちばしとくちばしを触れ合わせる。研究者たちは、この小さな儀式が、ひなを育てるという困難な仕事が始まる前に、絆を強める助けになっていると考えている。

私たちはいつか必ず死ぬ存在であり、それはアホウドリも免れないことだが、それでも私たちはしばしば、人間の忠誠心を、これらの鳥が自分たちの忠誠心を扱うよりもずっと下手に扱っている。私たちは漂い、比較し、他の相手のほうがもっと刺激的だったのではないかと考える。誰にも分かる限り、アホウドリはそのどれもしない。ただ、毎年毎年、同じ狭い崖と、同じ見慣れたくちばしのもとへ戻ってくるだけだ。

これは、献身が盲目的であるべきだとか、疑問を持たずにいるべきだという意味ではない。だが、献身を、一度の劇的な誓いとしてではなく、季節ごとに、実際の代償を払いながら、死ぬまで静かに繰り返される習慣として扱う生き物から、学ぶべきことがある。私たちは自分たちをより進化した種だと呼んでいる。この特定の点においては、証拠は必ずしも私たちの側にあるわけではない。
