Script

Last weekend, four of us decided to explore the coast by bicycle. None of us owned bikes, so we went to a small rental shop near the station. Outside, dozens of bikes stood neatly lined up in a row, and choosing one felt strangely exciting.

The shop was popular, so we had to line up for almost twenty minutes just to fill in the forms. The owner explained the rules carefully: we were expected to return the bikes by five o'clock, and we had to wear helmets the whole time. It all felt very organised.

The ride itself was wonderful. We followed a quiet path beside the sea for hours, stopping whenever we found a nice view. I had not cycled in years, and honestly I forgot how tiring it can be.

The next morning, I paid the price. My legs were so stiff that I could barely walk down the stairs, and my shoulders ached from gripping the handlebars. My friends felt exactly the same, and we spent the whole day complaining and laughing about it.

Would I do it again? Absolutely. A little stiffness is a small price for a day like that. I have already promised myself we will rent bikes again next month.
