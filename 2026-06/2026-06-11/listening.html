Script

Last Saturday morning, I signed up for a bread-making class at a small kitchen studio near my flat. I had been meaning to go for months but kept putting it off. When I arrived, I realised quite quickly how much I did not know.

The instructor was calm and experienced, and she had a helper who moved quietly around the room making sure everyone had what they needed. The whole setup felt unexpectedly luxurious — clean wooden surfaces, good lighting, and every ingredient pre-measured and ready.

What the instructor kept coming back to was the importance of feel. She said a sense for the dough — knowing when it has been kneaded enough — is something common to all experienced bakers, but it is very hard to teach directly. She told us to keep in mind that no two batches behave in exactly the same way, and that learning to read the dough takes time.

At one point, a grown-up in the group — probably in his late fifties — asked whether there was a shortcut to getting the feel more quickly. The instructor smiled and said there was not really, but that after a few sessions most people find their instinct starts to improve.

By the end, we each had a small loaf to take home. Mine was a little uneven, but it tasted better than I expected. I have already looked up the next available session.
