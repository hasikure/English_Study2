Script

Last Sunday, I spent a few hours at an antiques fair in the town hall car park. I had not been to one before and was not sure what to expect.

The stalls were set up in rows, each one packed with old furniture, ceramics, and objects I could not always identify. I quickly learned to pay attention to the condition of anything I picked up — scratches, chips, and cracks were easy to overlook at first.

At one stall, a man was selling what he described as a Victorian relic — a small brass compass in a worn leather case. He said it had been carried on a trading ship, though he admitted he had no documents to prove it. The story gave it a momentary appeal that I found hard to resist.

The seller told me the asking price was forty pounds, preferably in cash. In practice, most traders at the fair accepted only cash, so I was glad I had stopped at a cash machine on the way.

I bought the compass, along with a slim illustrated guide to antique navigation instruments. I carried them home carefully and placed them on the shelf above my desk. I cannot be certain the compass is as old as the man suggested, but I enjoy looking at it all the same.
