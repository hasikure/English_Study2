Script

Last weekend, I went to a craft market held every month in a large building near the river. The building was formerly a printing works, and you can still see some of the old machinery displayed along the walls as decoration. I find that kind of thing interesting — places that have managed to get over their original purpose and become something completely new.

The market was more crowded than I had expected. Near the entrance, I found the noise hard to tolerate, so I moved toward the back, where it was quieter. There were stalls selling handmade pottery, second-hand books, and food from countries I have never visited.

At one point, I spoke to a woman who had been selling handwoven scarves there for three years. She told me the market had grown quite a lot since she first joined. I asked whether she ever covered up her more unusual patterns when she thought customers might not appreciate them. She laughed and said she used to, but she had stopped. Her most unusual designs, she explained, sold faster than anything else.

The whole experience reminded me that success in a market — whether a craft stall or anything else — is relative. What works in one place might not work somewhere else, and the only way to find out is to try.

I left with a small ceramic bowl and a bag of spiced nuts. It was not a cheap afternoon, but it was a good one.
