Script

Last Tuesday, I had a free afternoon and took a bus out to a country house about twenty minutes from town. I had driven past it many times but never bothered to go inside. The house was formerly owned by an aristocracy family and had been open to the public for about thirty years.

When I arrived, a guide was explaining how the house had been used during the Second World War. With so many men at work in factories or serving overseas, the rooms had been converted into a temporary hospital. She pointed to a black-and-white photograph showing nurses standing in one of the upstairs corridors.

What surprised me most was a rooftop terrace with a clear view of the surrounding farmland. The guide mentioned that the family had used it to watch for aircraft, and that on one occasion a helicopter had landed in the field below — though she admitted she was not certain that detail was accurate.

On the way home, I looked the house up online and found that it has become globally known among historians for one particular reason: an almost complete set of household accounts going back four hundred years. Apparently it is something of a treasure for researchers interested in how aristocracy families managed their estates.

I had not planned much for the afternoon, but it turned out to be one of the more interesting things I have done in a while.

到着すると、ガイドが第二次世界大戦中にその屋敷がどのように使われたかを説明しているところだった。多くの男性が工場で働いていたり海外に従軍したりしていたため、部屋は一時的な病院に転用されたという。ガイドは、上階の廊下に立つ看護師たちを写した白黒写真を指差した。

最も驚いたのは、周囲の農地が見渡せる屋上テラスだった。ガイドは、一家が戦時中に航空機を監視するためにそこを使っていたと話し、ある時ヘリコプターが下の畑に着陸したこともあったと語ったが、その詳細が正確かどうかは確かではないと認めた。

帰り道にオンラインで調べたところ、その屋敷は400年前まで遡るほぼ完全な家計の記録で世界的に歴史家の間で知られていることがわかった。貴族の一家がどのように領地を管理していたかに関心のある研究者にとって、ある種の宝物だという。
