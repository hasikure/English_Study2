Last Thursday, I went to a careers fair at the local college. I had been thinking about changing direction for a while, and a colleague mentioned that one of the speakers might be worth listening to.

The hall was busier than I expected. I found a seat next to a young woman who was taking careful notes. She told me she was a final-year student still deciding between staying in the country and working abroad.

The main speaker was introduced as a retired diplomat who had spent over twenty years working in Asia and the Middle East. He spoke slowly and precisely. He seemed prepared to answer even the most difficult questions without losing his composure. I found myself leaning forward to catch every word.

After the talk, a spokeswoman for the college gave a brief summary of support services available to students and career changers, and directed people to a table near the exit. There, a seller of career guides and reference books had set up a small stall. I bought a slim book on international careers and read the first few chapters on the way home.

It was a more useful afternoon than I had expected. I still have not decided what to do next, but I left feeling less stuck than when I arrived.
