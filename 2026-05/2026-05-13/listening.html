On Monday afternoon, I started my part-time job at a small cafe inside the city bus terminal. Behind the counter, pale wallboard held a handwritten menu of drinks, soup, and daily specials in neat white letters. Because it was my first shift, my manager, Mr. Kim, stood beside me and showed me how to greet people, smile, and take a customer's order without making them wait too long.

At first I was nervous, but the customers were kind. One elderly woman even told me I had a clear voice. My coworkers also helped a lot. Sara explained how to use the cash register, and Diego showed me where the extra cups were kept. I was relieved that I could get along well with both of them.

Near the end of the shift, a man made an allegation that we had charged him twice. Mr. Kim checked the machine and found only one payment. The man apologized, and the line moved again. After that, Mr. Kim thanked me for staying calm. I told him I was indebted to him for his patience and for giving me a chance to learn. When I left the terminal that evening, I was tired, but I felt proud of my first day.

最初は緊張していたが、お客さんたちは親切だった。年配の女性のひとりは、私の声が聞き取りやすいとまで言ってくれた。同僚たちもとても助けてくれた。サラはレジの使い方を説明してくれ、ディエゴは予備のカップがどこにあるかを教えてくれた。私は、二人とすぐにうまくやっていけそうだと感じて安心した。
