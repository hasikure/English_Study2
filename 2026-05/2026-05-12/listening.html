Script

On Sunday morning, I visited a rooftop garden above an old apartment building downtown. My neighbor, Carla, had promised to show me around before the monthly garden market began. The roof was larger than I expected. There were tomato planters, climbing beans, and a corner full of yellow flowers. At first, I felt a moment of humiliation because I mixed up basil and mint in front of everyone. Carla only laughed and said that beginners often do that.

A little later, she asked me to lift up a heavy bag of soil while she opened the storage box. Then we arranged small pots on a long wooden table. By ten o'clock, the sun was already strong, and we worried that we might run out of iced tea before the visitors arrived. Still, nobody complained. The volunteers showed enduring patience and seemed honestly glad to help one another.

When the first families came through the gate, Carla stopped her work to show a young boy around. He wanted to grow strawberries on his balcony, and he listened carefully to every tip. I left the garden tired but cheerful. What stayed with me most was not the city view, but the simple pleasure of people growing food together.
