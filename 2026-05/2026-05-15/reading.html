English Text

Many driving schools still spend most classroom time on traffic rules and penalties. Those facts matter, but beginner drivers also need training in how to see. I believe driving courses should include short visual observation drills, because real roads change too quickly for people to hang on to a list of rules in their heads.

First, observation drills teach students to notice details under pressure. An instructor can show a street photo for three seconds, start a stopwatch, and ask simple questions: Where was the child standing? Which door was open? How close was the bicycle to the truck's bumper? This is not a game. In bad weather, the road can become such a confusing picture that even a careful learner misses a major risk. Through a wet windshield, lights and shadows sometimes look more like impressionism than like a clean diagram in a textbook.

Second, these drills create habits that transfer to daily driving. After each image, the teacher can add a short caption explaining the hidden danger: "The scooter is in the blind spot," or "The parked car may move suddenly." Because the explanation is brief, students can remember the lesson without feeling buried under information. Some instructors vigorously defend longer lectures, but a ten-minute drill often leaves a stronger mark than an hour of passive listening.

Finally, visual practice helps anxious students. Many beginners know the rules but freeze when several things happen at once. Timed exercises give them a safe way to practice scanning mirrors, sidewalks, and signals in order. One student in my local driving course said that she stopped staring at the car directly ahead and began checking the whole scene. That small change may prevent a crash more effectively than memorizing another page of regulations.

For these reasons, driving schools should treat observation as a core skill, not an extra topic. Safe driving depends not only on what we know, but also on what we notice in time.

第二に、こうしたドリルは日常の運転に移る習慣を作ります。それぞれの画像の後で、教師は隠れた危険を説明する短いキャプションを加えることができます。「スクーターは死角に入っている」「駐車中の車が急に動くかもしれない」といったものです。説明が短いので、生徒は情報に埋もれた気分にならずに、その教訓を覚えていられます。長い講義を力強く擁護する教官もいますが、10分のドリルのほうが、受け身で1時間話を聞くより強く印象に残ることが少なくありません。

最後に、視覚練習は不安の強い学習者にも役立ちます。多くの初心者はルールを知っていても、複数のことが同時に起こると固まってしまいます。時間を区切った練習は、ミラー、歩道、信号を順番に確認する練習を安全に行う方法になります。私の地域の教習コースにいたある生徒は、前の車だけを見つめるのをやめ、場面全体を確認するようになったと言っていました。その小さな変化は、規則をさらに1ページ暗記することよりも、事故防止に効果的かもしれません。

こうした理由から、教習所は観察を補助的な話題ではなく、中核的な技能として扱うべきです。安全運転を左右するのは、私たちが何を知っているかだけでなく、それに間に合う形で何に気づけるかでもあるのです。
