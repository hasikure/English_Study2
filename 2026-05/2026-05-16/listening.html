Yesterday evening, I joined a free bicycle repair class at the community sports center. About ten people stood around a long workbench covered with helmets, tire levers, and oily cloths. Our instructor, Mr. Ruiz, placed a small metal nut in my hand and showed how one loose part could make an entire wheel shake. He asked us not to wait quietly for help, but to take the initiative and test each bike by ourselves.

At first, I felt nervous. I had never touched the gears or brakes on a bicycle before. But Mr. Ruiz had a calm voice and a gift for persuasion. He said that learning one practical skill could save money, reduce waste, and give us more confidence in daily life. His words worked on me.

When I finally fixed a brake cable and spun the wheel, it turned smoothly. A woman next to me gave a small gasp because her own bike had the same problem. Soon we were helping each other, passing tools back and forth, and asking simple questions. By the end of the class, I had learned a great deal of useful information, and I rode home feeling proud.
