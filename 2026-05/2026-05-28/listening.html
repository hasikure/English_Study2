Last weekend, my family went to a local agricultural show held at a farm about thirty minutes from home.

The morning was slightly chaotic. We arrived too early, and the car park was still being set up. Volunteers in orange jackets were fuming at each other over which gates to open first, though they sorted it out within minutes.

Once inside, the variety of events was impressive. Near the main ring, a farmer demonstrated the mastery he had developed over forty years of working with sheepdogs. He gave a series of quiet whistles, and three dogs moved a flock of sheep across the field with almost no visible effort on his part.

In one corner, two teams were competing in a tug-of-war. The final pull lasted nearly three minutes and felt more like a duel than a team sport — the rope barely moved for the first two minutes before one side suddenly gave way.

My younger nephew spent most of the afternoon riding donkeys near the paddock fence. He was so pleased with himself that he refused to leave when it was time to go.

We got home late, but no one seemed to mind.

中に入ると、イベントの種類の多さに圧倒された。メインリングの近くでは、牧羊犬と40年間向き合ってきた農家の男性が、その熟達した技を披露した。彼は一連の静かな口笛を吹くだけで、3頭の犬が彼の側からはほとんど見えない動きで羊の群れをフィールド越しに移動させた。
