Yesterday, I visited a small mountain hospital with my cousin Emi, who works there as a nurse. The hospital was holding an open day for high school students who wanted to learn about grown-up jobs. As we walked up the hill, we saw an eagle circling above the trees, and Emi joked that it was checking our homework.

Inside, a doctor showed us how a single germ can move from one surface to another if people forget to wash their hands. We put bright powder on our palms and then touched a desk, a pen, and a door handle. In only a minute, the powder was everywhere.

At noon, the catering team brought simple lunch boxes for the visitors. By pure coincidence, the man serving tea recognized my family name. He had gone to school with my father many years ago, and he remembered him at once.

Before we left, Emi said that every job in the hospital mattered, from surgery to cleaning to catering. I had expected the day to feel serious and distant, but instead it felt warm and human. For the first time, a hospital seemed less frightening and more like a place where careful people quietly protect the town.
