Script

Last weekend I visited my uncle's new house on the outskirts of town, an old stone cottage he bought mostly because he liked the idea of fixing it up himself. Most of the walls looked solid, but the mortar between the stones near the chimney had crumbled badly over the decades, leaving gaps you could fit a finger into. I couldn't resist posing a question I had been wondering about the whole drive over: how does anyone even learn to repair something that old? He laughed and said he had spent the last two winters taking weekend courses from a retired mason in the next village, and had even started to earn a small income on the side repairing similar walls for neighbors once word got around. While we were mixing a fresh batch of lime mortar in his yard, he asked, almost as an afterthought, whether I happened to know anyone looking for help on weekends, by any chance, since he already had more requests than he could handle alone. I told him I would ask around, though honestly, watching him work that afternoon, carefully packing mortar into century-old gaps with a trowel, made me wonder if I should just learn to do it myself.
