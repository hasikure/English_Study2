Script

During my only real mountain race, I found myself matched against a much stronger opponent who had finished the course twice before and clearly knew every turn by heart. For the first hour, the altitude did not bother me much, but by the time we reached four thousand meters, every breath felt shorter than the last, and I started falling behind. The hardest part of the route was a long, open glacis just below the summit ridge, a wide, gently sloping bank of loose rock with nowhere to hide from the wind. It was there that our guide suddenly stopped and told us to wait: she had detected a change in the clouds moving in from the west, the kind of shift that usually means a storm within the hour. My opponent and I, who had spent the whole morning trying to beat each other, suddenly realized we were in the same boat, two tired climbers facing the same mountain and the same weather. We crossed the glacis together, checking on each other every few steps, and reached shelter just as the first hail started falling. I never did find out who technically won that day, and for the first time, I genuinely did not care.
