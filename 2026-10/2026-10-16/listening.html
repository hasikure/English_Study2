Script

Last Friday, our school unveiled a new reading garden, built in memory of Mr. Harlan, a science teacher who had worked there for almost thirty years before he passed away last spring. A group of students stayed after school for most of the term to help plan and plant it, working in conjunction with volunteers from the community center down the street. I'll admit the whole project almost didn't survive its first week: on the very first afternoon of digging, we all heard a loud bang from the parking lot that sounded exactly like a gunshot, and half the students dropped their tools and ran toward the building. It turned out to be nothing more than a delivery truck backfiring loudly as it pulled away, but for about ten seconds, I don't think anyone breathed. Once everyone calmed down and realized what had actually happened, we laughed about it for the rest of the afternoon, and oddly enough, it became something of a running joke among the volunteers for the rest of the project. By the time the garden was finished, there was a small bench near the entrance with Mr. Harlan's name on it, and underneath, a short line explaining that it had been planted in memory of someone who always told his students that curiosity was the only subject that mattered.
