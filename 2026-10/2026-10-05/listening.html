Script

Every morning before work, I warm up with a short stretch on my balcony and then go for a run that takes me past the old medieval bridge near the river, the one tourists always photograph. The route used to end at a busy intersection where cars, bicycles, and pedestrians all seemed to arrive at exactly the same moment, and for years I held my breath every time I crossed it. Last month, the city finally installed a proper traffic light there, and a spokesman for the local council explained that the change had been planned for almost a decade but kept getting delayed because of budget problems. When residents first heard about the construction, many of them reacted rather negatively, worried that the new lights would ruin the medieval character of the square and slow down traffic even further. I will admit I had my own doubts at first. But after running through that intersection every day for a month, I can say the opposite has happened: crossing feels calmer, drivers actually stop, and the square somehow feels more alive now that people are not sprinting across it in fear. I still warm up on the same balcony and run past the same bridge, but that one corner of my route no longer fills me with dread, and I suspect most of my neighbors would admit, however reluctantly, that the spokesman was right.
