Script

My aunt has run the same small bakery for almost twenty years, and her cinnamon bun recipe has remained completely unchanged since the day she opened. She manufactures everything in a kitchen barely bigger than a closet, mixing the dough by hand every morning before sunrise. Last year, in consideration of several regular customers who had become allergic to nuts, she finally agreed to create a nut-free version of the same bun. I happened to be visiting the week she first tested it, and I will never forget the agony on her face when the first batch came out of the oven looking perfect but tasting completely wrong, missing some flavor she couldn't quite name. She tried again the next morning, and the morning after that, refusing to simply add more sugar as a shortcut. By the fourth attempt, she had found it: a touch of cardamom that replaced exactly what the nuts used to contribute. When she finally let me taste the new bun, I honestly could not tell it apart from the original. Customers who tried it later said the same thing, and several admitted they only trusted her word that anything had changed at all. Watching her work that week taught me that keeping something feeling unchanged sometimes takes far more effort than quietly changing it would have.
