Script

When my brother and I decided to set up our own food truck, everyone warned us about the long hours, but nobody mentioned how long it would take before we actually got down to business. We spent nearly two months arguing about the menu before we served a single customer. In the end, our menu consists of just four items: two kinds of tacos, a soup, and one dessert, kept deliberately small so we could actually make everything well. A friend who had already run a food truck for years told us that the best predictor of whether a new truck survives its first year is not the food itself but how consistently the owners show up, rain or shine, even on days when almost nobody comes. We have definitely felt the toll of that advice: six-day weeks, four in the morning starts, and more dishwashing than either of us expected. Still, watching a regular customer come back for the third week in a row, specifically asking for the same taco, makes the whole thing feel worth it. We are nowhere near rich, but for the first time in years, we are genuinely excited to get up and get down to business every single morning.
