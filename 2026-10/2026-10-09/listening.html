Last month I tried to buy a used car from a small dealership near my apartment, and the whole experience taught me to trust my instincts more. The salesman promised to provide me with a full service history before I signed anything, and at first he seemed happy to fulfill every request I made. But when I asked for the paperwork a second time, he kept changing the subject, and I started to feel suspicious. A friend who used to work as a mechanic came along to look at the car with me, and within ten minutes he noticed something almost invisible: a thin line of fresh paint along one door, hiding a repair that had never been mentioned. When I brought it up, the salesman's story changed again, and I nearly had words with him right there in the parking lot. In the end, I walked away without the car, and I am glad I did. A few weeks later, a coworker told me the same dealership had sold her a vehicle with a similar hidden repair, one that left her stranded on the highway within a month. I still need a car, but now I always bring someone who knows what to look for, and I no longer assume that a friendly salesman will automatically fulfill his promises just because he sounds sincere.
