Script

This weekend, my sister suggested we finally sit down and plan our parents' anniversary party, since the guest list has been on the agenda for weeks without anyone actually deciding anything. My brother said he'd rather stay in and watch the game, but once my sister reminded him dessert was involved, he agreed to come after all, and my cousin added, only half joking, "and me, don't forget me." We ended up gathered around the kitchen table with coffee and a strange bottle of vanilla extract my mother insisted on showing everyone, since apparently a single drop can lighten the flavor of almost any dessert without making it taste artificial. Somewhere between arguing about seating and agreeing on a playlist, the whole evening stopped feeling like a chore and started feeling like the party itself had already begun. My brother, who had planned to stay in for maybe twenty minutes before sneaking back to the television, ended up staying for three hours, laughing harder than any of us expected. By the time everyone left, we had an actual plan, a shared folder of music, and, somehow, a renewed appreciation for vanilla extract. Planning a party, it turns out, can lighten more than just a cake.
